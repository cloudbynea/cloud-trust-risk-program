Cloud Risk Heatmap
Quarterly Cloud Health Review

Cybersecurity SaaS | AWS Environment

Purpose of This Heatmap

This heatmap provides an executive-level view of the most material cloud risks impacting platform resilience, enterprise trust, audit readiness, and revenue protection.

Risks are evaluated not by technical inconvenience, but by:

• potential business impact
• blast radius across the organization
• recoverability of failure scenarios
• regulatory and contractual exposure

The objective is to enable leadership to make prioritized, outcome-driven decisions about cloud governance and investment.

Risk Evaluation Model

Each risk domain is assessed across three dimensions:

Business Impact
Effect on customer trust, contractual obligations, regulatory exposure, and revenue continuity.

Recoverability
Ability of the organization to regain operational and administrative control after a failure event.

Likelihood
Probability of occurrence given current controls, operating patterns, and growth trajectory.

This framework supports consistent prioritization and longitudinal tracking across quarterly reviews.

Critical Risk Domain
Root Account & MFA Fragility

Severity: Critical
Likelihood: Medium
Business Exposure: Organizational survivability, regulatory exposure, executive accountability

Context

Root account and MFA controls represent the highest-authority trust boundary within the cloud environment.

Loss, compromise, or mismanagement of these credentials introduces non-recoverable failure scenarios, including the inability to assert administrative control, preserve forensic integrity, or reliably recover service ownership.

Leadership Framing

This domain represents a small technical surface with disproportionately high business consequences.

Failure conditions at this level are not operational incidents.
They are organizational risk events.

Outcome Risks

• prolonged or irreversible service disruption
• loss of administrative sovereignty
• breach of regulated data environments
• audit failure
• erosion of enterprise customer confidence

Strategic Implication

Root governance must transition from technical stewardship to executive-mandated control with formal enforcement mechanisms.

High Risk Domains
Privileged Access Sprawl

Severity: High
Likelihood: High
Business Exposure: Breach probability, audit readiness, enterprise contract confidence

Context

Administrative and high-privilege access is distributed across multiple teams without consistent ownership models, periodic validation, or centralized approval frameworks.

This expands the effective attack surface and increases the probability of lateral movement following credential compromise.

Leadership Framing

Each additional privileged identity introduces compounding risk.

The exposure grows silently and is rarely visible in traditional operational reporting.

Outcome Risks

• increased breach probability
• weakened least-privilege claims
• audit and customer security review findings
• reduced incident containment capability

Strategic Implication

Privileged access governance must evolve from convenience-driven provisioning to policy-driven authorization with executive oversight.

Orphaned Identities (Former Employees)

Severity: High
Likelihood: Medium
Business Exposure: Compliance failure, unauthorized access, incident investigation gaps

Context

Former employee identities persist within the AWS environment beyond employment separation points, often outside active monitoring and validation cycles.

These identities represent undocumented entry points into sensitive systems.

Leadership Framing

This risk is not operational drift.
It is a standing compliance and audit exposure.

Outcome Risks

• SOC 2 / ISO nonconformities
• contractual assurance challenges
• unauthorized access scenarios
• impaired forensic traceability

Strategic Implication

Access lifecycle governance must be formally integrated into employment lifecycle processes with accountable ownership.

Moderate Risk Domains
Over-Permissioned Roles

Severity: Moderate
Likelihood: High
Business Exposure: Blast radius expansion, recovery complexity, remediation cost

Context

IAM roles and policies frequently exceed functional necessity, often due to inherited permissions, wildcard policy elements, and historical provisioning patterns.

While not always immediately catastrophic, these permissions materially increase the impact scope of any identity compromise.

Leadership Framing

These conditions function as blast-radius accelerators.

Outcome Risks

• deeper compromise pathways
• slower incident containment
• expanded forensic and remediation scope
• increased recovery cost

Strategic Implication

Policy hygiene and least-privilege baselining must become continuous governance processes rather than periodic cleanup activities.

Incident Response Maturity

Severity: Moderate
Likelihood: Medium
Business Exposure: Impact amplification, executive confidence, recovery velocity

Context

Incident response workflows are primarily dependent on individual expertise rather than standardized playbooks, automated containment capabilities, and defined executive communication protocols.

Leadership Framing

Process maturity does not prevent incidents.
It determines whether incidents become manageable events or organizational crises.

Outcome Risks

• delayed containment
• inconsistent executive messaging
• prolonged customer impact
• avoidable reputational damage

Strategic Implication

As preventative controls strengthen, incident response must mature into a structured executive operating capability.

Executive Interpretation

The current cloud posture reflects a technically capable organization entering a governance-dependent stage of maturity.

Without leadership-level ownership models, enforcement authority, and visibility mechanisms, risk will continue to scale faster than the organization’s ability to manage it.

This heatmap establishes the foundation for:

• structured prioritization
• executive decision-making
• and longitudinal tracking across quarterly health cycles

Each risk domain is mapped directly to remediation streams within the 30-60-90 day Cloud Leadership Roadmap, ensuring alignment between risk identification, executive accountability, and operational execution.
